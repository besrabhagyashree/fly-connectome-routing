## Gap: what has not been done, and what our project could do

1. **Inside-only (boundary-locked) shuffles exist in exactly one study.** Park 2026 (#21) used them on a *compressed* FlyWire graph (512 groups) with *evolved* foraging agents. Nobody has run them on the **full-resolution FlyWire v783 graph**, on a **fixed reservoir with a trained readout**, or on a **classic game (Tetris, chess, Pong, Snake)**.
2. **Every other wiring control is a "standard" shuffle.** These include degree-preserving swaps, weight reassignment, presynaptic-ID shuffles, and random graphs (#1–4, 6–9, 18, 19, 22, 23). Park's result shows that standard nulls move the sensory→motor boundary, and that this alone can create or remove a wiring "advantage". So the positive results here are all open to a boundary confound: FlyTris 2×, fly-cartpole 500 vs 166, Minnick 95% vs 18%, FlyGM, and Cho 92.8% vs 67.9%. So are the nulls: ChessFly, alpha-fly, tic-tac-toe, FlyAim, gate course. None of these tested whether the effect survives a boundary-preserving null.
3. **No fly-connectome Tetris or chess project compares real vs inside-only shuffled wiring.** The Tetris projects (#1, #16–18) and chess projects (#2–5) either have no control, a standard shuffle, or a designed control with no results. The larva Tetris repo (#18) is the only Tetris reservoir with a control, and it is unfinished.
4. **The opening for us:** one pre-registered benchmark: FlyWire v783 as a fixed reservoir with a trained readout, on one or two games (e.g. Tetris and a gym task). It would have three arms, real vs standard shuffle vs inside-only boundary-locked shuffle, each with matched training budget and multiple seeds. It would directly test whether the claimed "wiring advantages" in this hobby wave are interior computation or boundary artefacts. This would extend Park's evolutionary finding to the reservoir/readout setting, which nobody has done.
